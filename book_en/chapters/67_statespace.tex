\chapter{State-Space, Exponential Smoothing and Mixed Models}

\section{Unobserved Components}

`ucm` decomposes a series into a trend, a seasonal, a cycle, and an
irregular component, each with its own variance.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={UCM}]
let m_ucm = ucm(df, y)
print(m_ucm)
\end{lstlisting}

The model is useful for seasonal adjustment and for extracting a latent
business-cycle component.

\section{Exponential smoothing}

`ets` fits an Error-Trend-Seasonal model; `hwes` or `holtwinters`
provides the Holt-Winters additive variant; `ses` is simple exponential
smoothing.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Holt-Winters}]
let m_hw = holtwinters(df, y, trend="add", seasonal="add", period=12)
print(m_hw)
\end{lstlisting}

The command estimates the smoothing parameters ($\alpha$, $\beta$,
$\gamma$) and reports the fitted level, trend, and seasonal components.

\section{Mixed models}

`mixed` or `mixedlm` fits a linear mixed model with random intercepts
and/or random slopes. The fixed effects are interpreted as in `fe` or
`re`, while the random effects capture unobserved heterogeneity.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Linear mixed model}]
let m_mixed = mixed(y ~ x1 + x2, df, id="school")
print(m_mixed)
\end{lstlisting}

The output reports fixed-effect coefficients, variance components, and
empirical-Bayes predictions of the random effects.

\section{Kalman and structural time series}

`kalman` (Chapter~54) and `ucm` are the main state-space tools.
Additional structural commands include:

\begin{itemize}
\item `msauto` / `markov_ar` for Markov-switching autoregressions
\item `structural_ts` for generic structural time-series
\end{itemize}

These models are estimated by maximum likelihood or quasi-MLE. Check the
reported log-likelihood and information criteria when comparing
specifications.
